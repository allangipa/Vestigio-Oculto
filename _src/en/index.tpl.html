✓ Method

When there is no answer, the dossier says so.

Each case brings the chronology before the theory, both ends of the debate with the weight of evidence stated, and the sources at the end. No mystery is closed for convenience — and no improbable thesis is dismissed by decree: it is answered with the material from the site itself.

How the archive is organized

Four fronts, four ways a mystery holds out.

An enigma survives for different reasons: because the stone is too old, because the technology only arrived now, because the paper was never read, or because no one could get close.

Stone and Dust

Architecture and engineering that do not fit the accepted chronology. What was built before there was anyone who, in theory, knew how to build it.

{{CONTAGEM:pedra}}

Discovered Yesterday

Finds that only appeared because technology changed. Lidar, tomography, sequencing — new instruments rereading old evidence.

{{CONTAGEM:ontem}}

Sealed Archive

Undeciphered documents and expeditions without an ending. Cases in which the record exists, is preserved — and remains illegible.

{{CONTAGEM:selado}}

The Last Isolated

Territories and peoples beyond the reach of the contemporary world — and what our curiosity costs them.

{{CONTAGEM:isolados}}
Criteria

What goes into a dossier and what does not.

01 Chronology before theory. Dates, coordinates and who was present come first. Interpretation comes after, identified as interpretation.
02 Both ends of the debate. The academic reading and the conspiratorial one appear side by side, with the weight of evidence for each stated.
03 Generated images are labeled as such. Where no photograph of the site exists, the re-creation comes flagged. It never passes for a record.
04 Entirely original writing. The sources are listed at the end of each dossier; the text is written from scratch, never recycled.
